printf prints inline. do println for newline, or add /n inside the string

"auto" when declaring a variable makes a copy of whatever it's referencing. to directly reference the object, do "auto&"

.h files are the menu, they tell you what exists (verbs, function declaration etc)
.cpp files are the kitchen, they determine what is INSIDE the functions etc.

so main.cpp will use my abstracted, platform-agnostic verbs, which are defined in input.h, and the input_core2.cpp for the core2 version of the game will "hear" the calls for the verbs and respond with the core2 button inputs and what they should do

static means "private to this file"

for includes, angle brackets mean libraries and quotes mean "one of my files" - the path starts from /src/
